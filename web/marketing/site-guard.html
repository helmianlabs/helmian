What it stops

The dangerous classes never reach the AI.

Site Guard judges every message against named classes. A message that lands in one of the dangerous classes is blocked outright, and the block is recorded under its class name.

01

Destructive asks

Asks that would talk the AI into damaging, deleting, or exposing something it should never touch.

Blocked outright

02

Fishing for secrets

Asks for private information, credentials, or the site's own secrets, asked plain or dressed up as something else.

Blocked and named

03

Prompt injection

Attempts to replace the AI's instructions with the visitor's own, or to smuggle private content out in a disguised form.

Blocked before it lands

How it works

Screened first. Answered second.

The screening sits in front of the AI, on the site's own road. Nothing a visitor sends reaches the AI unscreened.

  1. 01

    The message arrives

    A visitor sends a message to the site's AI: a chat panel, a search box, a form, a voice line.

  2. 02

    Site Guard judges it

    Patterns are matched and scored against the named classes. Over the block line, the message stops at the door. In the middle band, the message goes through and the flag lands in the owner's feed.

  3. 03

    The record is written

    Every verdict that matters leaves one feed entry: the class, the verdict, and the source fingerprint. Never the visitor's words.

Records

Proof of the decision. None of the words.

Site Guard's records are built to be shown to an auditor. They carry patterns and verdicts, and content-bearing fields are refused by name.

The record proves what was decided.

A feed entry names the class, the verdict, and the fingerprint of the source. It never repeats what the visitor said, and secrets are stripped from anything the site sends onward before it leaves.

If it is not a pattern or a verdict, it is not in the record.
